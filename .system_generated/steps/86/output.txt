### **Core Local AI Infrastructure**
To run AI models locally, you need a "runtime" (or engine). The two industry leaders for Mac users are:

*   **Ollama:** The professional choice for automation and development. It runs as a lightweight background daemon, integrates easily with tools like `curl` or Python for custom scripts, and is generally faster and more memory-efficient.
*   **LM Studio:** The best choice for a polished, graphical interface. It is ideal if you prefer visual model management, tweaking parameters via sliders, and having a "ChatGPT-like" experience without touching a terminal.

### **Upwork Freelancing & AI Automation**
When automating your Upwork workflow, it is critical to stay within their Terms of Service.
*   **What is allowed:** AI can be used for drafting proposals, organizing project information, proofreading, and brainstorming.
*   **What is NOT allowed:** Fully automated bots that submit proposals without human intervention. Upwork has strict detection for automated browser scripts; any proposal must be reviewed and sent by you personally.
*   **Best Practice:** Use AI to generate high-quality, personalized cover letters based on the job description, but always perform a final review and manual "send."

### **Hardware Considerations for 2026**
Performance in 2026 is gated by **Unified Memory (RAM)** rather than CPU core count.
*   **8GB RAM:** Suitable only for very small, lightweight models (e.g., 1B–3B parameter models).
*   **16GB RAM:** The practical minimum for a professional developer or freelancer running modern 7B–9B parameter models (e.g., Qwen 3.5, Llama 3).
*   **36GB+ RAM:** Recommended if you intend to run larger models (32B+ parameters) or process massive local document repositories using RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation).

### **Privacy & Safety**
Running tools locally ensures your client data, proprietary code, and sensitive documents never touch a third-party server. When selecting tools, prioritize those that offer "Local-First" or "On-Device" processing. Always keep **FileVault** enabled on your MacBook to ensure your local data remains encrypted at rest.
