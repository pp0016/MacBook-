The MacBook Air M5, released in March 2026, features a fanless design that inherently leads to thermal throttling under sustained heavy workloads[1][2][3]. Because it lacks active cooling, the system is designed to manage heat by reducing clock speeds once internal temperatures reach critical thresholds[4][3][5].

### Sustained Performance & Thermal Throttling
*   **Behavior Under Load:** Like its predecessors, the M5 MacBook Air is designed for bursty performance[3][6]. During short tasks, it performs exceptionally well and matches the M5 MacBook Pro[3][5]. However, during sustained tasks (e.g., long gaming sessions, 4K video rendering, or intensive AI processing), the chip will heat up and throttle to remain within thermal limits[7][8][3][9].
*   **Thermal Impact:** In stress tests, temperatures can reach high levels (e.g., ~94°C–107°C) before the system forces a reduction in performance[8][9]. Users have reported that after a few minutes of intensive load, performance may drop by approximately 20% to 30% to stabilize[7][8]. Once it reaches this "throttled" state, the performance typically plateaus and remains stable for the duration of the task[4][7].
*   **Mitigation:** Some users have found that placing the laptop in a vertical, open-lid configuration or using external cooling aids (like a small fan or cooling stand) can help maintain higher performance levels for longer periods by improving heat dissipation[4][5].

### Benchmark Context
*   **Performance Retention:** Under sustained AI or compute-heavy loads, the M5 MacBook Air typically settles at roughly 70–75% of its "cold" (peak) benchmark score[7]. While this drop is measurable in benchmarks, many users note that for "daily" tasks and even moderate gaming, the performance remains smooth and the throttling is not always perceptible in real-world usage[4][3][5].
*   **Comparison:** If your workflow requires consistent, long-term peak performance without throttling, the MacBook Pro series with active cooling remains the recommended choice, as it can sustain higher clock speeds indefinitely by exhausting heat through its fans[3][6][5].

In summary, while the M5 MacBook Air is significantly faster than the M4 model, its fanless nature means it is not optimized for prolonged "redlining" of the GPU or CPU[10][7][3]. It excels at tasks that allow the machine to idle or cool down between bursts of activity[7].
