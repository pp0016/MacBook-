The Apple M5 series of chips was released throughout 2025 and 2026, manufactured using TSMC’s third-generation 3-nanometer (N3P) process[1][2].

### **Release Dates**
*   **M5 (Base):** Introduced on **October 15, 2025**, for the 14-inch MacBook Pro and iPad Pro[3][1][4].
*   **M5 Pro & M5 Max:** Announced on **March 3, 2026**, alongside updated MacBook Pro models[5][6][7].
*   **M5 Ultra:** Introduced on **August 25, 2026**, alongside an updated Mac Studio[5][8].

### **Specifications Overview**
The M5 series utilizes Apple’s "Fusion Architecture" for higher-end models, which combines multiple dies into a single System on a Chip (SoC)[1][6][2].

| Chip | CPU Cores | GPU Cores | Memory (RAM) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M5** | 9 or 10 cores | 8 or 10 cores | 12, 16, 24, or 32 GB[1] |
| **M5 Pro** | 15 or 18 cores | 16 or 20 cores | 24, 48, or 64 GB[1] |
| **M5 Max** | 18 cores | 32 or 40 cores | 36, 48, 64, or 128 GB[1] |
| **M5 Ultra** | 30 or 36 cores | 64 or 80 cores | 96, 256, or 512 GB[1] |

### **Key Technical Details**
*   **Transistor Count:** While Apple often highlights performance gains, specific official transistor counts for the entire M5 family have been widely cited in technical discussions as approximately **28 billion** for the base M5 chip[9].
*   **Architecture:** The M5 series features a new CPU architecture incorporating "Super Cores" for high-performance single-threaded tasks and performance cores for power-efficient multithreaded workloads[6][2].
*   **AI Performance:** All M5 variants include a 16-core Neural Engine and, notably, integrate **Neural Accelerators** within each GPU core, providing a significant boost to on-device AI and machine learning tasks[3][10][7].
*   **Memory:** The chips use LPDDR5X memory with speeds up to 9600 MT/s[1].
