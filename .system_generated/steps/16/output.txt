The Apple M5 chip series, introduced starting in October 2025, features a significant architectural shift with the introduction of "super cores" and the integration of Neural Accelerators within the GPU[1][2].

### "Super Cores" Architecture
The term **"super core"** was adopted to describe Apple’s highest-performing CPU core design, which was originally referred to as the "performance core" in the base M5 model[1][3]. 
*   **Purpose:** These cores are designed to provide the world’s fastest single-threaded performance[4][5].
*   **Technical Enhancements:** This performance boost is achieved through increased front-end bandwidth, a redesigned cache hierarchy, and enhanced branch prediction[1][6].
*   **Heterogeneous Design:** The M5 family uses a mix of core types. For instance, the base M5 typically pairs these super cores with efficiency cores, while Pro and Max variants incorporate a combination of super cores and a new, distinct "performance core" design optimized for power-efficient multithreaded workloads[1][3].

### GPU Neural Accelerators
A major highlight of the M5 generation is the advancement in on-device AI compute capabilities[4][7].
*   **Per-Core Integration:** Each GPU core in the M5 architecture includes a dedicated **Neural Accelerator**[1][8].
*   **Functionality:** These accelerators are programmable units that support FP16 and INT8 matrix operations[8]. They allow the GPU to handle intensive AI tasks—such as running local Large Language Models (LLMs), image generation, and real-time analysis—dramatically faster than previous generations[6][9][7].
*   **Performance:** This architecture allows the GPU to contribute significantly to total AI throughput, working alongside the system's dedicated 16-core Neural Engine (NPU) to provide a substantial increase in peak AI compute compared to the M4 series[8][7].

### Summary of Core Configurations
| Chip Variant | CPU Structure (Examples) | GPU Cores |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **M5 (Base)** | 4 Super Cores + 6 Efficiency Cores[10][2] | 8 or 10 Cores[10][2] |
| **M5 Pro** | 5-6 Super Cores + 10-12 Performance Cores[11] | 16 or 20 Cores[11] |
| **M5 Max** | 6 Super Cores + 12 Performance Cores[2][11] | 32 or 40 Cores[2] |
| **M5 Ultra** | 12 Super Cores + 24 Performance Cores[12][2] | 64 or 80 Cores[2] |

This modular, multi-die "Fusion Architecture" allows Apple to scale these configurations effectively across the M5 family, from the MacBook Air up to the workstation-class M5 Ultra[12][13][2].
